2. Course content and objectives

  • Description
    • Spanish phonetics, phonology, and morphology. Exercises in transcription. Contrastive analysis of English and Spanish sound systems.
  • Goals / expectations
    • Students will
      • investigate the structure of speech and its linguistic function in the Spanishes of the world;
      • gain experience with the articulation of sounds by producing and transcribing speech patterns;
      • accurately define and use terminology related to phonetics and phonology in English and Spanish;
      • mark syllabic stress patterns and describe basic anatomical structures used in articulation;
      • transcribe sounds using IPA and IPA-related symbols; and
      • recognize the processes represented in transcribed or spoken words.
    • 585 students will
      • prepare a project appropriate to their discipline
        • MAT: 5 lessons of a portfolio unit
        • MA T/I: project on T/I relating to sounds
        • PhD: a paper aimed at their research goals

  • PP
    • The oral PowerPoint presentation (PP) will consist of a 10-minute explanation (in Spanish) of either a phonetic/phonological phenomenon of the Spanish language or an anomaly in the sound system of a dialect of Spanish, for example, of Jayuya, Puerto Rico, of Ciudad Juárez, Mexico, of Bilbao, Spain, of Costa Rica, of Equatorial Guinea, etc. The report will indicate sounds that vary from a 'norm,' and will be based on research done in the library and on the internet. The grade for this presentation will be based on proper oral production, 'grammar and stylistics,' and content.
      • grading of presentation
        • content = 5%, creativity/interest generated = 2%, presentational style/intangibles = 3% for a total of 10%
      • 362 students may work in pairs, but the presentation should last 15 min.
    • The presentations begin on date listed on calendar below, and the professor will choose who goes first, unless there are volunteers.
  • OP
    • Students will record from a written script (the so-called oral productions, or OP) so that the professor can identify problems or characteristics in pronunciation (native speakers do not have the same variations that non-natives have, but may 'vary' nonetheless).
    • Students should use their own computer to record the passage.
      • There are online apps such as voki.com and others, which may assist in the passage preparation.
      • Place each recording in Dropbox or Voice Thread by the date indicated in the course calendar.
      • Read and record the text in a normal voice.
      • The grade will be based on
        • the amount of variation from the "norm", i.e. "mispronunciations" occurring from interference (e.g., [æ] for [a]) or dialectal variation (e.g., elision of -s or -d-); and
        • the percentage of improvement in pronunciation.
  • Students must check the online syllabus regularly for updates.
  • Students will find some material for the course online.
